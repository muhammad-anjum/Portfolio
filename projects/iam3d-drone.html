01 · Design brief

Build for a mission, not just for flight.

The competition required an FPV racing drone to navigate an obstacle course while carrying and releasing a small payload without manual intervention. As Project Manager, Muhammad directed the four-part project plan, coordinated meetings, documentation, procurement, budget, and travel, and kept the team’s research, design, manufacturing, and testing work moving together.

02 · System selection

Use trade studies to keep performance, compatibility, and cost in view.

The team weighted candidate components against mission needs rather than selecting them independently. The documented configuration paired a SpeedyBee F405 V4 BLS 55A stack with a Tattu R-Line 1550 mAh 4S battery, MEPS SZ2207 2750KV motors, DJI O3 video hardware, and a Crossfire-compatible radio link.

$496.56 totalThe design report’s bill of materials covered the flight system, payload hardware, electronics, and printed structure.
03 · Payload release

Reject the obvious mechanism when its failure mode is wrong.

An energize-to-hold electromagnet was dropped because it would draw power continuously and create heat during flight. The selected concept paired a passive magnet with servo-driven linear motion: a lead screw created the separation needed to release the payload without depending on continuous electrical holding force.

04 · Camera alignment

Iterate toward the smaller, clearer mechanism.

A dual-camera ground-station concept was rejected for cost and control complexity. The team then explored mechanical camera orientations, eliminating a geared mechanism when it consumed too much volume and interfered with wiring. A bottom-pivot mount preserved the required motion with less material and a simpler load path.

05 · Battery restraint

Design against the movement the vehicle will actually see.

Rather than relying on straps alone, the battery plate added grooves and raised edge walls to resist fore-aft sliding under acceleration. Grip tape and a single strap provided retention without multiplying hardware.

06 · Lightweighting

Remove mass without designing past the structure.

SolidWorks Simulation and Altair Inspire framed the optimization work. At the planned 40% motor-throttle limit, the analysis used a 5.072 N load per motor and a 0.5 mm displacement limit. Topology studies at multiple mass targets informed a manually refined, printable frame that projected a 27% mass reduction while staying within that limit.

07 · Design for printing

Let the manufacturing process shape the geometry.

Carbon-fiber-reinforced PLA and a Bambu Lab X1 Carbon supported the team’s print strategy. A dedicated clearance test established 0.15 mm as the free-moving fit for printed interfaces. The frame was oriented flat to avoid supports and preserve layer adhesion; the magnet cage was oriented so its layers carried the expected shear more effectively.

08 · Verification & result

Keep the report honest about where validation stood.

The design report documents component checks, electronics setup, mechanism development, and structural analysis, but it was completed before the team’s flight-test program concluded. After that report was issued, the project progressed to competition and finished fourth internationally.
