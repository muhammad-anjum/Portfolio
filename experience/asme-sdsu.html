Where my responsibility started and ended.

What I led

  • Directing every phase of the chapter’s four-part project plan, with milestones and owners for each.
  • Organizing and leading general-body and project meetings.
  • Implementing the project-tracking and design-documentation tools.
  • Procurement and budgeting for materials, tools and travel.
  • Coordinating build sessions, testing, travel and competition preparation.

What the team engineered

  • The component decision matrices and the payload, camera and battery mechanisms.
  • The topology optimization, finite-element and harmonic analyses.
  • Print orientation, clearance testing, printing and assembly.
  • The electronics build and flight-control setup.
  • The design report, which lists me as Project Manager.

I coordinated the people doing that work and kept the project moving. I did not design every component. The case study covers the engineering and credits it to the team.

One plan, four phases.

ResearchDesignManufacturingTesting

The design report describes the project in these four phases. Research narrowed a wide field of FPV components to a few candidates per part through decision matrices. Design produced the payload, camera and battery mechanisms and the optimized frame. Manufacturing meant repeated prints and the electronics bring-up. Testing was the phase the schedule squeezed.

Manufacturing and the electronics build ran at the same time, so several people owned different parts of the plan at once.

How the work was managed.

Requirements to plan

Translated the competition brief into the four-part plan, with phases, milestones and owners, and directed each phase through to competition.

Meetings

Organized and led the chapter’s general-body meetings, along with the meetings specific to the drone project.

Between subteams

Coordinated between the technical subteams, because the payload cage, camera pivot, battery plate and frame all had to fit one another. The case study shows where those interfaces sat.

Tracking and documentation

Implemented the project-tracking and design-documentation tools. The 38-page design report, with its decision matrices, calculations and analysis, is the documentation that came out of that system.

Procurement and budget

Managed procurement and budgeting for materials, tools and travel, and tracked deadlines. The report documents a $496.56 bill of materials.

Build, test and travel

Coordinated build sessions, testing, travel and competition preparation.

Risks and blockers

Monitored project risks across the subteams and worked to clear blockers. The table below lists the risks the report documents and how the team responded.

Staying aligned

Kept the team aligned through failures and late-stage repairs. The photographs on the case study show a damaged aircraft in the field and repair work in a hotel room during the trip.

Risks the project carried.

RiskHow the team responded
The rulebook did not say whether custom electronics were allowed, and follow-up inquiries did not settle itThe team dropped the electromagnet designs and used a passive magnet with a commercial servo for release
No payload weight was givenThe team estimated 22.23 g and sized the magnet at about four times a conservative retention requirement
The goggles accept one video sourceThe team designed a mechanical camera pivot instead of adding a second camera
Printed parts could breakThe team printed spare parts to make sure a complete flight was possible
Time ran short for flight testingThe report records the tests as pending, with a mock course planned; the aircraft then went on to competition

Decisions with a record.

The team’s trade studies put criteria and weights on paper before parts were chosen, so a decision could be reviewed later. Where a part scored higher on paper and the team chose another, such as the flight-controller stack picked for compatibility, the reasoning went into the design report.

The report names me as Project Manager, and the plan, schedule and documentation were my responsibility. The engineering decisions belong to the team that made them.

Requirements, trade studies, the payload mechanism, structural analysis, printing and the competition result.
