Build the community students were missing.

Muslim students interested in technology at SDSU needed a stronger professional community on campus: direct access to professionals, practical career preparation, collaborators on technical work, and peers with shared experiences.

There was a second need nearby. Local Muslim organizations have technical needs that student skills can meet, and a chapter could be the link between the two.

Muslim Tech Collaborative is a national organization of Muslims and allies in technology, structured in tiers from national leadership to regional hubs, university chapters and youth chapters. It supplied a mission and a model. It could not supply local leadership. The programming, the relationships and the day-to-day execution had to be built at SDSU. The national organization explains its structure here.

Turn a mission into a chapter that can run.

Founding the chapter took more than a name and a first meeting. I defined what the SDSU chapter should provide, built the leadership team and divided responsibilities, recruited members, and developed the chapter’s identity and calendar. I directed club operations: organization, outreach, marketing, recruitment and funding strategy.

Behind each event is logistics: rooms, schedules, promotion, and working relationships with companies, professionals and community institutions. Funding and employer partnerships support the panels and workshops. The goal throughout was to build processes that keep working after one academic year.

Four kinds of work, one chapter.

Career development

Résumé-review workshops, career-preparation sessions and professional panels give students a way to test their materials and plans against what working professionals look for. Each format is meant to leave a student with something to act on: a revised résumé, a clearer picture of a role, a person to follow up with.

  • Résumé-review workshops with practical feedback
  • Career preparation for internships and full-time roles
  • Professional panels
  • Conversations with working professionals

Industry access

Company information sessions, industry panels and joint company tours put students in the same room as people doing the work, and show them more than one technical and professional path. Our chapter hosted an LPL Financial x MTC SDSU informational session in October 2025, covered in the next section.

  • Company information sessions
  • Joint company tours
  • Industry panels
  • Relationships with professionals and company representatives

Community and belonging

Not everything the chapter does is career-driven. Social events and regular contact give Muslim students interested in technology a place to meet and support each other, across majors and years. Those relationships are what keep members involved after a single event or recruiting cycle.

  • Social events
  • Peer relationships across majors
  • A campus space for Muslim students interested in technology
  • Connections that last beyond one event

Community technology and service

The chapter works with local masajid and Muslim community organizations to find volunteer opportunities and to match student skills with community needs. Some of that work is building products, such as websites, for organizations that may not have a technical team of their own.

This is the part of the chapter that points outward: it helps students find opportunities, and it gives them a way to use their skills for others.

  • Volunteer opportunities with local masajid and community organizations
  • Websites and other technical products for community partners
  • Support for organizations without dedicated technical teams

What I owned, and what the chapter did together.

Strategy

I defined what the SDSU chapter should provide, translated the national mission into campus programming, and set priorities that balance career development, community, technical work and service.

Team leadership

I built and coordinated the leadership team: delegating event and project responsibilities, keeping officers and volunteers aligned on deadlines and commitments, and supporting members who wanted to lead programs or projects.

Program development

I planned the chapter calendar and chose formats to fit what students needed, so that résumé workshops, panels, socials, tours and service initiatives are different kinds of events rather than one format repeated.

External relationships

I communicated with professionals and company representatives, worked with the national MTC organization, represented the chapter to partners and local community organizations, and secured the funding and employer partnerships behind our panels and workshops.

Execution and operations

Our team reserved spaces, coordinated speakers and schedules, prepared materials, and handled promotion and attendee communication. I oversaw setup and event flow and followed up afterward, so the chapter could run events consistently.

Community projects

I helped scope technical work for community partners, matched student capabilities to what organizations needed, and coordinated delivery and communication. Members built the products.
